3. The Rules

1. Completed actions in the past

Use the preterite for completed past actions. Match the form to the subject: yo, tú, él/ella/usted, nosotros/nosotras, vosotros/vosotras, ellos/ellas/ustedes. Usted uses the él/ella form; ustedes uses the ellos/ellas form.

2. Irregular stems

Learn these stems: traer → traj-, andar → anduv-, saber → sup-, venir → vin-, decir → dij-, poner → pus-, estar → estuv-, tener → tuv-, querer → quis-, hacer → hic-. Add -e, -iste, -o, -imos, -isteis, -ieron. These forms have no written accent marks.

3. Special forms: hacer, decir, traer

Hacer changes hic- to hiz- in él/ella/usted: hizo. The j-stems use -eron: dijeron and trajeron, without an i.

4. Ser and ir share all six forms

Fui, fuiste, fue, fuimos, fuisteis, fueron. Context tells you which verb is meant: El viaje fue tremendo (The trip was tremendous); Yo fui a Costa Rica (I went to Costa Rica).

5. Dar and ver

Dar: di, diste, dio, dimos, disteis, dieron. Ver: vi, viste, vio, vimos, visteis, vieron. These short forms have no written accent marks. Ver follows regular -er preterite endings without the accents on vi and vio.

6. Find the subject, even with nos

Nuestros profesores nos dijeron: the professors are the subject, so use ellos → dijeron. Nos means “to us”; it does not make the subject nosotros. Carmen nos dio su número: Carmen is the subject, so use ella → dio.

7. Meaning in context

Saber in the preterite often means “found out”: Usted supo la noticia. Tener que means “had to”: Tuve que estudiar. No querer in the preterite can mean “refused”: María no quiso ir al baile.

8. Practice with pronouns

Subject pronouns can be omitted when clear. The quiz asks for the verb alone. English “you” may correspond to tú or usted; learn both. Vosotros/vosotras means informal plural “you,” mainly in Spain.
